Chromosphere Density Check

Is the Fuller & Tsuna (2024) time-averaged chromosphere, built from our MESA red supergiants, denser than the extended atmospheres actually observed around Betelgeuse and Antares? The grain-growth runs suggested it is: in that density, efficient silicate dust formation overproduces the wind by 1 to 3 orders of magnitude.

  • Radius convention matters. Harper, Brown & Lim 2001 use the 11.15 µm diameter (56 mas), 1.32 times the near-IR photospheric diameter (42.5 mas). Their radii are rescaled to the photospheric scale here, which is what the model's R means. Without that rescaling the observed densities would look another 3–4× lower at 2 R*.
  • Distance. Radio free-free opacity scales as n_H² × path length, so n_H ∝ d^(−1/2): moving Betelgeuse from 131 to 222 pc lowers the Harper densities by 0.77.
  • Clumping. Radio data measure ⟨n_H²⟩, so the smooth density they imply is an upper bound on the mean. Clumping makes the model look even denser by comparison.
  • Uncertainty. About a factor of 3 from distance and radius, the metal ionisation fraction (n_H ∝ x_e^(−1/2)) and epoch variability (radio temperatures changed between 2019 and 2026). The Antares MOLsphere value is uncertain by about 10× (geometry and chemistry).

Bottom line

  • The model chromosphere is too dense at 1.5–3 R* by about 3–5× against golden16 as built, and 2–12× when scaled to Betelgeuse, depending on distance and radius. The excess is largest near 2 R*, falls to about 2× at 4–5 R* where the observed profile steepens (∝ r^−2.85), and reaches 30–50× in the Antares MOLsphere near 1.25 R*.
  • The model's mass-loss rate is too high by the same order (5–10×). A 10–25% lower convective velocity, about 6–7 km/s instead of MESA's 8.2, would bring the profile onto the observations, because the density is exponential in v_esc/v_con.
  • The observed mean outflow inside 5 R* is under 5 km/s, so this gas is mostly quasi-static or turbulent.
Grain-growth runs with the density lowered (×0.3 to ×0.01, and v_esc/v_con from 11.4 to 20) are still running. They will show which density and grain efficiency reproduce the observed 1e-7 to 1e-5 Msun/yr at 10–40 km/s. This page will be updated with them.
