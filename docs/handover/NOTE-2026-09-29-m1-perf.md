# NOTE 2026-09-29 (DeltaAI): implicit M1 speed-up, branch m1-perf-0928 — `implicit_opac_newton` now DEFAULT ON

**Result:** the 3-D He box (implicit grey M1 + vet_sc, hesdirk2, cfl 0.3) is **~60 % faster** at every GPU count
(GH200). Accuracy is unchanged, including second order in time and no ringing, and the other Newton-on/off checks
(Cartesian, sp, hydro, MHD) agree to solver tolerance. Branch `m1-perf-0928` = 401875f0 + the commits below.
Scripts and inputs: `docs/handover/m1-perf-0928/` (the job scripts use absolute DeltaAI paths
`/u/jma20/ATHENAK/bench-2026-09-29-hebox`; the copies here are for reference).

Why it was slow: under `implicit_opac_update` the Picard loop is a fixed-point iteration on kappa_T(T) of the face
terms and converges linearly (~0.17/pass). With the Newton term the converged state is the same (the added terms are
proportional to the gas step's predicted dT, which vanishes at the fixed point).

## Accuracy

- Tolerance (3259848): Newton at tol 1e-8 is closer to base at 1e-12 than base at 1e-8 in most hst columns.
- Time order from an EVOLVED restart (t = 40 → 80, cfl 0.6/0.3/0.15/0.075, full rst; jobs 3260169 base,
  3260196 Newton; `ord_hebox.py`): dens 1.97/1.89, mom1 1.85/1.85, ener 1.97/1.86, E 1.46/2.02 for BOTH;
  Newton = base to 3-4 digits in every entry; ||u_base - u_newton|| is 1e-4..1e-2 of the time error. (F1 and the
  transverse fluxes sit at the tolerance floor / outside the asymptotic range, identically for both.) A FRESH start
  is first order for both (start-up transient + BE first step).
- Ringing: odd-even content R <= 0.05 in every hst column, identical for base and Newton.
