THE HEADLINE: PHASE C's DIAGNOSIS WAS WRONG
================================================================================
Phase C blamed the divergence of the seeded 2-D He slab on the LAGGED OFF-DIAGONAL
Eddington terms.  Three runs of the seeded slab, three steps each, settle it:

  implicit_offdiag  implicit_closure_lag   Picard passes   final 7-point residual
  lagged (phase C)  pass (phase C)         200 / 200       3.0e3      DIVERGES
  none              pass                   200 / 200       1.6e3      DIVERGES
  operator          pass                   200 / 200       ~1e3       DIVERGES
  operator          STEP                   4 / 4           2.4e-15    CONVERGES
  none              STEP                   4 / 4           2.4e-15    CONVERGES
  operator          pass, closure_relax 0.3 200 / 200      6.6e2      DIVERGES

Dropping the off-diagonal terms ENTIRELY does not help; freezing the CLOSURE (chi and
the flux direction n) over the step fixes it with the off-diagonal terms left exactly
where phase C had them.  The divergent fixed-point iteration is the one on the CLOSURE:
each pass rebuilds the Eddington tensor from the flux of the iterate, and in an
optically thin cell an O(1) change of n changes the face flux by c E.  Under-relaxing
the closure between passes (implicit_closure_relax = 0.3, and 0.5 on thin faces only)
does NOT restore a contraction -- it only slows the walk.

WHAT PASSES.  The slab now RUNS: 200 s instead of 1.9 s, at 0.54 s/step instead of
11-13, with no dt collapse, bounded outer passes (12.75 mean) and a 7-point residual at
the tolerance.  The linear and the outer iteration are no longer the failure.

WHAT DOES NOT.  (a) NINE steps of 1393 still hit implicit_maxit -- the gate asked for
zero; the same nine appear with `none`, so they are not the off-diagonal terms.  (b) The
DYNAMICS ARE NOT SENSIBLE: KE_2 grows at 0.157 /s over the first 20 s, which is 75x the
convective rate v_MLT/H_p = 2.1e-3 /s, and by 200 s (0.42 turnover) the slab carries
max|v2| = 740 v_MLT, is supersonic (max|v|/c_s = 2.6), has lost 61 % of its emergent
flux and has F_rad/F_in(z) NEGATIVE in places.  No growth rate, no F_conv/F and no
velocity structure can be claimed from this run and none is: it is a SMOKE TEST that
says the solver survives, not a convection run.  (c) The positivity fallback fires on
1388 of 1393 steps, i.e. after its first pass nearly every step runs in `none` -- and
`none` is the mode G-oblique measures at 22 % error.  That the operator arm is
nonetheless BETTER than the pure-`none` arm in every column (half the early KE_2 growth,
1.5x the retained flux, less negative F) is the one physical argument in its favour.

WHAT THE NEXT SUSPECT IS, and what it is NOT.  It is not the linear solver, not the
lagged off-diagonal terms and not the closure iteration: all three are now converged to
1e-11.  The 2-D slab has two things the 1-D column does not: a TRANSVERSE radiative
force dm2 with no well-balanced reference (force_reference = wb_arad subtracts
rho a_rad_ref in x1 only) and no sponge of any kind.  The 0.16 /s early growth is in
KE_2, i.e. exactly there.

A THIRD ARM, `lagged` + closure STEP, BLOWS UP: dt collapses to 8e-33 at cycle 14 with
v = 4e38 cm/s.  So with the closure frozen the three modes order as
operator > none >> lagged, which is the phase-C mechanism (a lagged term amplified by
(c dt/dx)^2 in the thin top) showing up as an instability rather than as a
non-converging iteration.  This is why the positivity fallback goes to `none` and not
to `lagged`.
