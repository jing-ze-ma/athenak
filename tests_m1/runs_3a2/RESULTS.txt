THE CONSISTENT PAIR (marshak_q, q0).  The Marshak condition F = c q E_top fixes the
surface reduced flux to f(0) = q = <rad_m1>/marshak_q EXACTLY; nothing else in the
problem determines the integration constant.  Hence
       q0 = chi(marshak_q)/marshak_q,   and for marshak_q = 1/2,
       chi(1/2) = 0.46481586,  q0 = 0.92963172.
chi(f)/f has a minimum 0.89806 at f = 0.82504; q0 is above it, so the solution stays on
the decreasing branch at every tau >= 0.

TARGET (E within 1 % everywhere including the top 5 cells): MET by `central` with a
factor 7 to spare, at every CFL, and MISSED by a factor 40 by every upwind form.
WHY: the face-eliminated central flux has NO numerical dissipation, while the upwind
HLL flux carries 0.289 c dx, which exceeds the physical c/(3 rho kappa) wherever
tau_cell < 1.15 and then sets the gradient of E itself.  The berthon column is
systematically too bright below tau ~ 20, reaching +37 % at tau = 0.3 (the profile is
in plots/impl2_atm_berthon.json).
The explicit scheme's 49 % surface error is its DARK GHOST: it lets f -> 1 at the free
surface instead of imposing f = 1/2, which the implicit face-flux Marshak boundary does
exactly.

CONCLUSION.  LIMIT 1 and LIMIT 2 pull in opposite directions.  For the optically thick
stellar columns this milestone exists for, LIMIT 2 is the one that matters, so
implicit_flux = central stays the RECOMMENDED DEFAULT; berthon is for problems where a
thin front has to propagate, and is then 1.5x better than central at CFL 0.4 (2.2x with
plm_dc).
